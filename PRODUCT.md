# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Nuxt 3 with Vue 3, Tailwind CSS. Existing codebase; stack already established.

## Users

Primary: Prospective students at secondary school level considering Computer Programming studies at Nakhon Phanom Technical College. They want to understand the program, what they'll learn, what real projects and experiences await them, and whether this program aligns with their career interests.

Secondary: Parents researching the program to help their children make an informed decision about enrollment. Current students exploring opportunities and resources.

## Product Purpose

Provide a clear, realistic picture of the Computer Programming department through the lens of student experience rather than formal institutional description. Show the student journey—learning progression, real student projects, internship experiences, and future career pathways—so prospective students can imagine themselves in the program and make a confident enrollment decision.

## Positioning

The department differentiates itself by presenting authentic student work and practical experiences (projects, internships, pathways) rather than generic promotional content. The website builds trust through real student voices and demonstrated outcomes, helping visitors understand what actually studying here means.

## Operating Context

Visitors are typically researching educational programs on desktop or mobile during school counseling sessions, at home with parents, or during personal research time. The decision cycle spans days to weeks. Prospective students need quick understanding of program value; parents need reassurance about outcomes and quality. Content must work well on both desktop and mobile, with no performance friction.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Confirmed:**
- Thai-first content, designed for Thai students and parents. English/i18n not a current priority.
- Existing visual identity must be preserved: primary colors are blue, yellow, and white; interface must remain comfortable to read without being overly bright.
- Real student projects, internship experiences, and department photos should be used when available; layouts must work with placeholders during development until assets are collected.
- Each major section should have distinct visual character while maintaining cohesion across the site.
- Responsive design critical: desktop can be more expressive and interactive, mobile must remain clear and performant.
- Animations and interactions must be purposeful and supportive of content, not decorative.
- Avoid overusing card-based layouts or repeating the same pattern across sections.
- Existing institutional information and factual student experiences must not be fabricated; use clearly temporary placeholder content for incomplete information.
- Existing content structure and student journey are correct unless UX evidence suggests improvement.

**Platform/Accessibility:**
- No specific accessibility requirement established yet; standard web best practices apply.

## Brand Commitments

Department identity and Thai-first positioning are binding. Visual identity centers on blue, yellow, and white palette established in brand. Modern and approachable tone, avoiding generic AI-generated feeling or overly formal institutional presentation. Trustworthy voice grounded in real student experience.

## Evidence on Hand

- Existing Nuxt 3 codebase with established component structure: navbar, hero, about, learning sections, projects, internships, future paths, FAQ, footer.
- Existing Tailwind CSS setup and color tokens defined in project.
- Content structure aligns with student journey: learning → projects → internship → future paths.
- Real student projects and department photos to be sourced and added during implementation.

## Product Principles

1. **Authentic student voice over marketing speak.** Show real outcomes and experiences; build trust through demonstrated capability, not claims.

2. **Student journey as narrative spine.** Organize information around what students actually experience year by year or phase by phase, not administrative categories.

3. **Modern without generic.** Avoid templates and overdone patterns; each section should feel intentional and part of a cohesive whole.

4. **Accessible on all devices.** Ensure prospective students and parents can explore confidently on mobile, tablet, or desktop without friction.

5. **Respect user context.** Visitors are researching an educational decision; prioritize clarity and scanability over novelty or visual excess.
